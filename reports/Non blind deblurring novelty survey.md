# Kernel-family shift, not architecture, carries the novelty

Neither candidate method has a new core mechanism. Prior work already publishes each building block. FiLM-style affine modulation of a U-Net by a learned kernel embedding, with a ground-truth-kernel (non-blind) variant, appears in Kaufman & Fattal (CVPR 2020) ([arXiv 2004.02956](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.02956)) [V]. The closed-form step X=(conj(K)Y+ρX0)/(|K|²+ρ) is the data module of USRNet and DPIR. USRNet already learns ρ as a function of σ through an MLP trained end to end through the FFT ([arXiv 2003.10428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)) [V]. Unrolled HQS with a shared prior and an exact FFT data step at every stage is USRNet at s=1 [V]. What the searches did not find is a set of specific combinations: a NAFNet backbone FiLM-conditioned on a known kernel for non-blind deblurring; a direct regressor that ends with a single DC projection; an unrolled prior that receives the kernel embedding and stage index through feature-wise FiLM; and, most importantly, a controlled evaluation protocol in which held-out kernel *families* (disc, ring, ghost) are separated from held-out *parameters* and tested with paired per-image statistics. The project's headline observation (NAFNet loses about 2.8 dB on unseen kernels, USRNet about 0.4 dB) agrees with a well-established qualitative consensus. The literature has not reported it as a controlled measurement. Ring/annulus and ghost/double-exposure kernels do not appear as out-of-distribution families in any surveyed paper. The defensible paper is therefore "a controlled study of where the kernel should enter a non-blind deblurrer, and what that does under kernel-family shift", with K-NAFNet and the FiLM-HQS model as the probes. A paper claiming "first kernel-conditioned network" or "new DC formulation" would be rejected on prior art.

## Kernel conditioning has a seven-year lineage, mostly in super-resolution

Using the kernel itself as the network condition dates to **SRMD (CVPR 2018)**. SRMD projects the kernel by PCA to a 15-d code, appends σ, stretches the result to H×W maps and concatenates it at the first layer only ([arXiv 1712.06116](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06116)) [R]. **SFTMD/IKC (CVPR 2019)** moved the kernel code into Spatial Feature Transform affine modulation in every block and reported that this beats first-layer concatenation "by a wide margin" ([arXiv 1904.03377](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03377)) [V via search excerpt]. Later variants include dynamic convolutions (UDVD, KOALAnet), contrastively learned implicit degradation embeddings (DASR, KDSR, CDFormer), and type-level prompts or text (PromptIR, DA-CLIP, AutoDIR, and InstructIR, which uses a NAFNet backbone conditioned on a sentence embedding rather than a kernel) ([arXiv 2401.16468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16468)) [R]. Almost all of this work is super-resolution or all-in-one restoration, and kernels are almost always Gaussians.

The prior art that matters most for K-NAFNet is **Kaufman & Fattal 2020**. Their synthesis U-Net applies `r ← r ⊙ (1 + m(k)) + b(k)` at every layer. The terms m and b come from three FC layers on the raw flattened kernel. This is FiLM on the kernel in residual "1+" form, which is close to identity at initialisation. They report **28.28 dB with the ground-truth kernel, against 23.7 dB kernel-agnostic and 26.6 dB with an estimated kernel**, on 1000 synthetic camera-shake images ([arXiv 2004.02956](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.02956)) [V]. Their setting is uniform spline motion only, with separate networks per kernel-size class, and they compare against neither USRNet nor DPIR. The nearest modern analogues are blind. UFPNet plugs kernel attention into NAFNet ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Fang_Self-Supervised_Non-Uniform_Kernel_Estimation_With_Flow-Based_Motion_Prior_for_Blind_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)) [V, abstract/repo]. Sun et al. 2025 fuse kernel features by frequency attention. Their non-blind ablation gains only **+0.15–0.2 dB over their blind variant** (GoPro-Syn 28.59 → 28.80) ([arXiv 2504.14664](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14664v1)) [V]. **Converse-USRNet (ICCV 2025)** maps the kernel to a **64-d embedding via a three-layer MLP**. The embedding parameterises a closed-form reverse convolution, not FiLM ([arXiv 2508.09824](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09824)) [V]. That paper trains on 7×7 Gaussians and tests on 7×7 motion kernels without framing this as a generalisation study, and it reports no USRNet/DPIR rows. KBNet is a frequent false positive: its "kernel basis attention" learns convolution bases and is not conditioned on the blur kernel ([arXiv 2303.02881](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.02881)) [V].

On the noise axis, USRNet feeds σ through an MLP that emits per-stage scalars [V]. DPIR concatenates a constant noise-level map ([arXiv 2008.13751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751)) [V]. AdaFM/CResMD modulate on a scalar degradation level ([arXiv 1904.08118](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08118)) [R]. No reviewed paper uses a Fourier-feature embedding of log σ. That is a genuine but minor difference.

## The closed-form DC step is a 2020 template; its placement is the only new element

The Wiener-first family places the closed-form inverse *before* a network and sets its regularisation from image statistics rather than learning it. This family includes Schuler et al. 2013 ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2013/html/Schuler_A_Machine_Learning_2013_CVPR_paper.html)), Son & Lee 2017 ([POSTECH](https://remotecenter.postech.ac.kr/handle/2014.oak/42195)), DWDN, INFWIDE and MultiWienerNet. **DWDN** applies feature-space Wiener deconvolution with a statistics-derived noise-to-signal ratio. It reaches **36.90/32.77/30.77 dB on Levin at 1/3/5% noise**, against FNBD's 34.81/30.63/27.93, but never compares to USRNet or DPIR ([arXiv 2103.09962v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.09962v2)) [V]. **INFWIDE** adds an image-space branch. It has no inference-time DC, only a training-time reblur loss worth about 0.23 dB ([ar5iv 2207.08201](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.08201)) [V].

Interleaved designs put the same step *inside* the loop. FDN (ICCV 2017) interleaves FFT deconvolution with CNN regularisers ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2017/html/Kruse_Learning_to_Push_ICCV_2017_paper.html)); its exact formula is UNVERIFIED. DPIR's Eq. 9 is literally the proposed formula, with **α_k = λσ²/σ_k², λ = 0.23 hand-set** and the denoiser not trained through the FFT [V]. USRNet's data module reduces to the same formula at s=1 and has no trainable parameters, while its hyper-network H(σ, s) learns α_k end to end [V]. **DiffPIR** predicts x0 at each diffusion step and then applies exactly this FFT proximal with ρ_t = λ(σ_n/σ̄_t)² ([ar5iv 2305.08995](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.08995)) [V]. Structurally that is "network predicts x0, then one closed-form DC with ρ tied to σ", repeated over sampling steps and training-free. The MRI analogue is Schlemper et al.'s DC layer. There λ = q/σ "can be fixed or made trainable" and is trained end to end in a CNN+DC cascade ([arXiv 1704.02422](https://arxiv.org/html/1704.02422v2)) [V].

The defensible residue for K-NAFNet's DC head is therefore narrow. In USRNet and DPIR the final output comes from the prior or denoiser, not the DC projection. No retrieved deblurring paper ends a single-shot modern backbone with exactly one DC projection and supervises both the pre-DC and post-DC outputs. The accurate framing is "a Schlemper-style DC head (n_c = 1) on a kernel-conditioned NAFNet", equivalently "USRNet with K=1 and reversed order". Two caveats matter for evaluation. First, the FFT step assumes circular boundaries. Eboli et al. explicitly argue against exact FFT data steps because of boundary artefacts and use preconditioned Richardson iterations instead ([arXiv 2007.01769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01769)) [V]. Second, for kernels with spectral zeros (disc Bessel nulls, ring and ghost notches), a single DC step with small ρ can reintroduce noise at those frequencies. That is a hypothesis to test, not a published finding.

## Unrolled HQS with a shared prior is USRNet; the kernel-aware prior is the open piece

USRNet unrolls HQS for **K=8** stages. It shares one ResUNet prior across all stages ("all the prior modules share the same architecture and same parameters"), feeds that prior only z_k concatenated with a noise map, and lets the kernel enter only through the FFT data module ([arXiv 2003.10428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)) [V]. Its only stage ablation is a single point: **K=5 loses about 0.1 dB on Gaussian and 0.3 dB on motion kernels against K=8**. With 8 iterations it beats IRCNN's 30 by at least about 1 dB [V]. The USRNet paper "does not consider the deblurring experiments", so no s=1 PSNR exists in it [V]. DPIR supplies the prior-strength evidence. At the same 8-iteration HQS, swapping the IRCNN denoiser for DRUNet is "significantly better", because "the denoiser plays a vital role" [V]. Convergent-PnP work shows that DPIR's PSNR peaks early and degrades over long runs, so short-T truncation is part of why it works ([arXiv 2201.13256](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.13256); [arXiv 2110.03220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03220)) [S]. DECUN uses a 1,117-parameter filter prior and shows diminishing returns after 30 layers ([arXiv 2402.12872](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12872)) [V]. Its full text also disproves a search snippet that claimed a NAFNet-in-HQS paper existed.

Several pieces were not found: NAFNet as the prior inside unrolled HQS/ADMM for non-blind deblurring; feature-wise FiLM on stage index; and a shared-vs-unshared deep-prior ablation in deblurring. Kernel-aware priors inside iterative deconvolution do exist outside the USRNet line. Li et al. (ICCV 2021) feed a PSF map into a deep prior within model-based deconvolution of lens aberrations. They argue that one weight setting across PSFs "will either require a large model capacity or lead to an average performance" ([arXiv 2104.03078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03078)) [V, excerpt]. In blind SR, DAN unfolds with a kernel-code-conditioned restorer ([arXiv 2010.02631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02631)) [U]. In spectral imaging, DAUHST feeds per-stage degradation-estimated α_k and β_k into each stage ([arXiv 2205.10102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10102)) [S]. Reviewers will cite these three as mechanism precedents. Kernel-conditioning the prior also carries a risk the notes flag explicitly. DPIR's prior never sees the kernel, so it is family-agnostic by construction [V]. A prior FiLM-ed on a kernel embedding can overfit to training families. The kernel-FiLM vs σ-only-FiLM ablation is therefore a required control, not optional.

## Diffusion solvers sidestep kernels and never test kernel families

Zero-shot diffusion solvers almost all use one benchmark (the "DPS protocol"). It consists of FFHQ/ImageNet 256², a single 61×61 Gaussian (std 3), **one fixed 61×61 motion kernel** ("the same kernel is used across all experiments"), and σ_y = 0.05 ([DiffPIR](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.08995); [DAPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01521)) [verified]. On PSNR they roughly tie DPIR. On FFHQ, DiffPIR scores **27.36 vs DPIR 27.79 dB** for Gaussian and 26.57 vs 26.41 for motion, while roughly halving FID [verified]. On ImageNet, DPIR still wins Gaussian PSNR (23.86 vs 22.80) [verified]. DAPS reports 29.19/29.66 dB on FFHQ Gaussian/motion but has no DPIR row, so cross-paper gaps of 1.4–3.3 dB are not same-run comparisons [verified]. Costs range from 20 to 1000 NFEs (DDRM 20, DiffPIR 100, DPS 1000), against USRNet's 8 small-network stages [verified]. Among these solvers, DiffPIR is the documented case of an exact FFT proximal. DDRM and DDNM use SVD projections, and DDRM supports only separable kernels. DPS uses gradient guidance with "no strict measurement consistency projection step" ([arXiv 2209.14687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14687)) [verified].

The search found three things missing here. No diffusion paper evaluates on disc, ring or double-exposure kernels. No paper reports USRNet under the diffusion protocol, or diffusion solvers under the Levin/BSD protocol. No non-blind diffusion denoiser takes the kernel as a conditioning input; kernel-conditioned diffusion exists only in blind form, where the kernel is the diffused variable (Kernel-Diff, [arXiv 2312.02319](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.02319)) [snippet]. Operator mismatch is an active 2025–2026 topic, but it uses perturbed Gaussian/motion kernels, not cross-family shifts ([arXiv 2610.29102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29102)) [snippet]. A cheap DiffPIR or DPIR run on P2-extrap would make a useful kernel-agnostic control.

## Robustness evidence is qualitative, and nobody tests ring or ghost kernels

The consensus that supervised end-to-end nets fail on blur unlike their training blur is long-standing. Rosenbaum & Weiss (NeurIPS 2015) already contrasted generative priors with discriminative methods that need retraining "for each blur kernel" ([reviews](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2015/file/596f713f9a7376fe90a62abaaedecc2d-Reviews.html)). Gilton et al. show learned reconstructions degrade under even slight forward-model drift ([arXiv 2012.00139](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.00139)). Blur2Blur reports NAFNet "often yielding output mostly identical to the blurred inputs" on unseen blur, but that shift is blind and real-domain, not known-kernel ([arXiv 2403.16205](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.16205)). The closest prior art to P2-extrap is **Chen, Quan, Pang & Ji (IJCV 2022)**. They test motion-trained supervised nets (FDN, IRCNN, DWDN, CPCR, others) without retraining on Danielyan's five non-motion kernels and conclude that they "do not generalize well". Classical **EPLL reached 30.41 dB on Set12, within about 0.5 dB of the best supervised net** ([IJCV 2022 PDF](https://cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/blog.nus.edu.sg/dist/8/10877/files/2022/04/IJCV-2022-Deblur.pdf)). That study reports absolute OOD tables only, with no per-method in-distribution baseline, no NAFNet or USRNet, and no paired statistics. Robustness to *kernel error* is a separate thread: Vasu et al. 2018 ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/papers/Vasu_Non-Blind_Deblurring_Handling_CVPR_2018_paper.pdf)) and Nan & Ji 2020 ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Nan_Deep_Learning_for_Handling_Kernelmodel_Uncertainty_in_Image_Deconvolution_CVPR_2020_paper.html)). No published USRNet/DPIR kernel-mismatch study was found.

The standard benchmarks (Levin 2009, Lai 2016) supply fixed kernel sets, not family splits ([Levin](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/2009/levin2009cvpr-understanding); [Lai](https://faculty.ucmerced.edu/mhyang/papers/cvpr16_deblurring_benchmark.pdf)). None of the inspected papers runs paired significance tests; they report means and occasional "margin" columns. Shimron et al.'s "implicit data crimes" give a citable motivation: up to 48% artificial improvement in MRI from evaluating on processed data, which transfers to the case of testing on kernels from the training generator ([arXiv 2109.08237](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2109.08237)). The SR analogue of a generalisation benchmark is SRGA/PIES, which finds that DAN "can only well generalized within mild degradation region" ([arXiv 2205.07019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.07019)). On the GMM side, the hybrid forms found are: EPLL itself ([Zoran & Weiss](https://people.csail.mit.edu/danielzoran/EPLLICCVCameraReady.pdf)); Rosenbaum & Weiss's gating network, which predicts GMM component assignments at about 100× EPLL's speed ([NeurIPS 2015](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2015/file/596f713f9a7376fe90a62abaaedecc2d-Paper.pdf)); deep GSM priors in unrolled MAP ([arXiv 2103.07152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07152)); and EPLL with a learned patch denoiser plugged in ([torchEPLL](https://github.com/friedmanroy/torchEPLL)). The project's ADMM-EPLL baseline sits squarely in this lineage. No GMM-routed deep experts for deblurring were found.

## Conclusion

The literature splits along one fault line. One camp feeds the kernel into network features (SRMD, SFTMD, Kaufman & Fattal, UFPNet). The other confines it to an exact data term (USRNet, DPIR, DiffPIR). The two camps have never been compared under a controlled kernel-family shift. The project's two candidates sit on either side of that line, and the FiLM-HQS model even straddles it. Their value is therefore diagnostic rather than architectural. The paper should be built around the question "which placement of the kernel survives unseen families, and at what in-distribution cost?", and the novelty matrix tells reviewers up front that the parts are borrowed.

One finding should shape the experiment plan. The notes predict that kernel conditioning can be a liability out of distribution, because DPIR's kernel-blind prior is robust by construction. The FiLM-HQS model could beat USRNet on P2-interp and lose to it on P2-extrap. That outcome is still publishable under this framing; it is not a failure. The σ-only-FiLM ablation and the DC-on/DC-off pair are what make either result interpretable. Before submission, the high-value manual checks are "cited-by" searches on Kaufman & Fattal 2020, SFTMD and IJCV 2022, limited to deblurring. Those are the most likely places for an unseen 2024–2026 competitor to appear.
